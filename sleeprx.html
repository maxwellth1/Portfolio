SleepRx

A wearable sleep-assistance prototype that senses heart rate and uses a pillow-mounted speaker system to guide the user's perceived heartbeat toward a slower rhythm.

View on Devpost ↗

What it does

SleepRx uses a pillow with built-in speakers to simulate the sound of a heartbeat. The system produces a descending heartbeat pattern that the user follows toward roughly 50–60 BPM, with the goal of helping the user transition toward sleep.

Project Demo

The device attached to my wrist is the ESP32-based heart-rate sensor we built for SleepRx. The sensor measures the user's heartbeat, while the computer display shows the live heart-rate output being captured by the device.

How we built it

Heart-rate sensing

A custom sensor uses a light sensor and green LED to detect blood-flow changes and estimate the user's heart rate.

ESP32 transmitter

One ESP32 handles the heartbeat-sensing side and transmits the measured information.

ESP32 receiver

A second ESP32 receives the data and drives the audio side of the system.

Pillow speakers

The receiver is integrated with a pillow speaker setup powered by a battery and two speakers.

Engineering challenges

Two key components—the MAX30102 heart-rate/oximeter sensor and suitable speaker drivers—were unavailable. We therefore spent significant development time building our own BPM measurement approach and working around speaker-amplification limitations.

The final prototype used an integrated circuit to amplify the speakers, although the audio retained some digital distortion compared with a natural heartbeat.
